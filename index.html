Your friend was right

It’s the Columbia River Gorge

Thirty minutes east of Portland, one cliffside highway threads past a dozen waterfalls in ten miles — most of them a few steps from the car.

The scenic road your friend described is the Historic Columbia River Highway. Its showpiece, Multnomah Falls, drops 620 feet in two tiers beneath a stone footbridge. It’s the richest concentration of easy-access waterfalls in the country — which is exactly why it suits a group that wants big payoffs without big climbs.

On timing: the crowd permit for Multnomah runs only May 22–Sept 7, 2026. Arrive Sept 8+ and you skip it entirely — as the light turns golden and the crowds head home.
When to go

After school starts is the right call

Your instinct lines up with the best conditions of the year. The one real wildcard either month is wildfire smoke — keep the coast as a clean-air backup and check the forecast a few days out.

Skip

Mid–late August

Hottest, most crowded, peak fire risk — and paid permits required at Multnomah.

Okay

Sept 1–7

Lovely, but still permit season and Labor Day crowds on the trails.

★Best — target this

Sept 8–16

School’s back, crowds thin, warm & dry, golden light — no Multnomah permit needed.

Also good

Late September

Cooler, quieter, first fall color. A fine plan B if flights price better.

The open jaw

Fly in to Portland. Fly home from Bend.

You’d trade a longer drive for more scenery — so the route never doubles back. Pick up the car at PDX, drop it at Redmond (RDM), and point the whole trip one direction. Flip the Crater Lake switch to stretch it south to Medford.

The waterfall drive your friend promised

Work west-to-east, hopping between falls: Latourell (224 ft over orange-lichen basalt), Bridal Veil, Wahkeena, the mighty Multnomah, and roadside Horsetail. Nearly all are short paved strolls; anyone craving a real hike can add the Wahkeena–Multnomah loop while the others relax at the lodge café.

Before you book

Six things to lock in

Book the open-jaw flights

Search “multi-city”: ATL→PDX, then RDM→ATL (or MFR with Crater Lake). ~$450/person in September.

Reserve a one-way SUV

Pick up PDX, drop RDM/MFR. Both in-state, so the one-way fee stays modest. Roomy for 4 + luggage.

Lodging: Hood River + Bend

2–3 nights each, plus night one near the Gorge. Book early — weekends fill even after Labor Day.

Multnomah permit — only if before Sept 8

Free timed permit on Recreation.gov, 14 days out at 7am PST.

Check the smoke forecast

Glance at oregonsmoke.org a few days out. If it’s hazy inland, the coast is plan B.

Pack layers + a polarizer

The Gorge can be misty. A circular polarizer tames waterfall glare and deepens that lake blue.
