Your friend was right

It's the Columbia River Gorge — and the Waterfall Corridor.

Thirty minutes east of Portland, one old cliffside highway threads past roughly a dozen waterfalls in ten miles. Most are a few steps from the car.

The scenic road your friend described is the Historic Columbia River Highway (U.S. 30). Its showpiece is Multnomah Falls — 620 feet in two tiers, with a stone footbridge crossing right over its face. It's the best concentration of easy-access waterfalls in the country, which is exactly why it fits a group that wants big payoffs without big climbs.

Good news on timing: the crowd-control permit for Multnomah Falls only runs May 22–Sept 7, 2026. Arrive Sept 8 or later and you skip it entirely — while the light turns golden and the summer crowds head home.
When to go

After school starts is the right call. Here's the exact window.

You wanted to wait until school's back in — that instinct lines up perfectly with the best conditions of the year. The one real wildcard either month is wildfire smoke; keep the coast as a clean-air backup and check the forecast a few days out.

Day by day

Seven days, unhurried.

Short walks with outsized views, one waterfall drive that does the heavy lifting, and a buffer day so nothing feels rushed. Difficulty is marked on every hike.

1 Arrive

Land in Portland, drift into the Gorge

Pick up the SUV at PDX and ease east. Close the first day at Vista House on Crown Point — a stone rotunda on a 700-foot bluff with the Gorge opening out below. No hiking, huge reward, and a golden-hour welcome to the trip.

2 The corridor

The waterfall drive your friend promised

Work west-to-east along the historic highway, hopping between falls: Latourell (224 ft over orange-lichen basalt), Bridal Veil, Wahkeena, the mighty Multnomah, and roadside Horsetail. Nearly all are short paved strolls. Anyone craving a real hike can add the Wahkeena–Multnomah loop while the others relax at the lodge café.

3 Mt. Hood loop

A lodge, a volcano, and a perfect reflection

Climb the Mt. Hood Scenic Loop to Timberline Lodge — the 1938 WPA masterpiece on the mountain's shoulder (you'll recognize its face from The Shining). Then the trip's signature photo: the flat Trillium Lake loop, where Mt. Hood lays itself out mirror-still on the water.

4 McKenzie

Blue water, old-growth, two roaring falls

Cross the Cascades on the McKenzie Highway and walk the Sahalie & Koosah Falls loop — two thundering waterfalls linked by an easy forest path along impossibly clear water. Feeling spry? The turquoise Tamolitch Blue Pool is a longer but gentle add-on. Roll into sunny Bend for dinner.

5 High desert

Smith Rock & the Deschutes

Ochre canyon walls curve above a green river bend at Smith Rock — one of Oregon's great views. Take the flat River Trail along the water and skip the aptly named Misery Ridge. Afternoon: a short walk to Tumalo Falls, or an easy riverside stroll through Bend's Old Mill District.

Before you book

Six things to lock in.

Book the open-jaw flights

Search "multi-city": ATL→PDX, then RDM→ATL (or MFR if you add Crater Lake). ~$450/person in September.

Reserve a one-way SUV

Pick up PDX, drop RDM/MFR. Both in-state, so the one-way fee stays modest. Roomy enough for 4 + luggage.

Lodging: Hood River + Bend

2–3 nights each, plus night one near the Gorge. Book early — weekends fill even after Labor Day.

Multnomah permit — only if before Sept 8

Free-to-reserve timed permit on Recreation.gov, 14 days out at 7am PST. Go Sept 8+ and skip it.

Check the smoke forecast

Glance at oregonsmoke.org a few days before. If it's hazy inland, the coast is your clear-air plan B.

Pack layers + a polarizer

The Gorge can be misty in summer. A circular polarizer tames waterfall glare and deepens that lake blue.
