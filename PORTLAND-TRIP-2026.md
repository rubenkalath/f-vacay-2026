# 🌲 Oregon Waterfalls & Cascades Trip — Sept 2026

**Travelers:** You, your sister, and your parents (b. 1954 & 1958 — so ~68 & ~72 in 2026)
**Hiking comfort:** Up to ~5–6 miles, nothing steep or sketchy
**Style:** Scenic beauty + big "wow" photo moments, relaxed pace
**Length:** 7 days (2 travel days), open to a longer loop / different fly-out airport

---

## ✅ Your friend was right — here's what he meant

The "gorge with the scenic road and tons of waterfalls" is the **Columbia River Gorge**, and
the drive is the **Historic Columbia River Highway (U.S. 30) — the "Waterfall Corridor."**
In about a 10-mile stretch just 30 minutes east of Portland, you pass **a dozen waterfalls**,
most of them a few steps from the car. It's the single best waterfall concentration in the U.S.
and it's tailor-made for a group that wants big payoffs without big hikes.

The crown jewel is **Multnomah Falls** — a 620-ft two-tier falls with the iconic Benson
footbridge across its face. 📷 [photos](https://www.google.com/search?q=Multnomah+Falls+Benson+Bridge&tbm=isch)

---

## 🗓️ When to go — recommendation: **the week of Sept 8–16, 2026**

You wanted "after school starts." That instinct is exactly right, and here's the precise sweet spot:

| Window | Verdict |
|---|---|
| **Mid–late August** | ❌ Hottest, most crowded, peak wildfire risk, **and** you need paid timed permits for Multnomah Falls |
| **Sept 1–7** | 🟡 Good, but still permit season + Labor Day (Sept 7) crowds |
| **⭐ Sept 8–16** | ✅ **Best.** School's back, crowds thin out, weather still warm & dry, golden light — **and Multnomah's timed-permit requirement ends Sept 7**, so you skip that hassle entirely |
| **Late Sept** | 🟢 Also lovely, slightly cooler, first color starting |

**One honest caveat:** September is still wildfire season in Oregon. Smoke is the only real
wildcard — some years it's crystal clear, some years it drifts in. You can't book around it, but
you can **build in flexibility**: the Oregon Coast almost always has clean ocean air, so it makes
a great smoke-day backup. Check [oregonsmoke.org](https://www.oregonsmoke.org/) a few days out.

> 💡 **Permit note:** In 2026, Multnomah Falls needs a [timed-use permit](https://www.recreation.gov/timed-entry/10089144) (9am–6pm)
> only **May 22 – Sept 7**. Go Sept 8+ and you need nothing. If you do go earlier, book on
> Recreation.gov 14 days ahead at 7am PST. The Historic Highway (U.S. 30) itself needs no permit.

---

## ✈️ Route recommendation: fly the "open jaw" — into Portland (PDX), out of Redmond/Bend (RDM)

You said you'd happily fly out of a different airport for a longer, more scenic drive. **Do it.**
The best version of this trip is a **one-way arc** — no backtracking — that strings the Gorge, Mt.
Hood, and Central Oregon's waterfalls together:

```
ATL ✈ PDX → Columbia Gorge → Hood River → Mt. Hood → McKenzie waterfalls → Bend → RDM ✈ ATL
```

- **Fly in:** Atlanta → **Portland (PDX)**, ~5.5 hr nonstop (Delta).
- **Fly out:** **Redmond (RDM)** → Atlanta, 1 connection (via SEA/SLC/DFW/PHX). Saves you a
  ~3-hour backtrack drive to Portland at the end.
- **Rental car:** one-way, pick up PDX / drop RDM. Both in Oregon, so the one-way drop fee is
  usually modest — get a roomy SUV for 4 adults + luggage.

This route keeps **every hike gentle**, packs in the most waterfalls, and ends in Bend — a
relaxed, sunny high-desert town that's a soft landing before the flight home.

## 🧭 The 7-Day Itinerary (2 travel + 5 on the ground)

### Day 1 — Fly in, ease into Portland
- Land at **PDX** early-to-midafternoon. Pick up the SUV.
- Drive 30 min east to **Troutdale / Corbett** (gateway to the Gorge) or stay in Portland.
- **Sunset at Vista House / Crown Point** — a stone rotunda on a 700-ft cliff with a jaw-dropping
  view straight down the Gorge. Zero hiking, huge payoff, great first-night photo.
  📷 [photos](https://www.google.com/search?q=Vista+House+Crown+Point+Columbia+Gorge+sunset&tbm=isch)
- Dinner in Portland's food-cart / brewery scene if you want a city night.

### Day 2 — ⭐ The Waterfall Corridor (the day your friend promised)
Drive the Historic Columbia River Highway west-to-east, stopping at falls all in a row. Almost
all are roadside or a short paved stroll — perfect for your parents.
- **Latourell Falls** — 224-ft plunge over bright-orange lichen basalt; paved path to the base.
  📷 [photos](https://www.google.com/search?q=Latourell+Falls+Oregon&tbm=isch)
- **Bridal Veil Falls** — easy 0.6-mi round-trip to a pretty two-tiered falls.
  📷 [photos](https://www.google.com/search?q=Bridal+Veil+Falls+Oregon&tbm=isch)
- **Wahkeena Falls** — visible a 5-min walk from the lot.
  📷 [photos](https://www.google.com/search?q=Wahkeena+Falls+Oregon&tbm=isch)
- **Multnomah Falls** 🏆 — the big one. Walk to the Benson Bridge (0.4 mi, paved, some grade) for
  the classic shot. 📷 [photos](https://www.google.com/search?q=Multnomah+Falls&tbm=isch)
- **Horsetail Falls** — literally drops beside the road.
  📷 [photos](https://www.google.com/search?q=Horsetail+Falls+Oregon&tbm=isch)
- **Optional for the fitter of the group:** the **Wahkeena–Multnomah Loop** (5.2 mi, ~1,600 ft
  gain) links several falls — beautiful but genuinely uphill, so it's a "maybe" for the parents.
  Easy alternative: everyone does the roadside falls; whoever wants can do the loop while the
  others relax at the Multnomah Falls Lodge café.
- Overnight in **Hood River** (charming windsurfing town on the river).

### Day 3 — Mt. Hood Scenic Loop
- Morning: **Panorama Point** above Hood River for orchard-and-mountain views.
  📷 [photos](https://www.google.com/search?q=Panorama+Point+Hood+River+Mount+Hood&tbm=isch)
- **Timberline Lodge** — the 1938 WPA lodge on Mt. Hood's shoulder (you may recognize its
  exterior from *The Shining*). Free to walk in, wander, and photograph the peak up close.
  📷 [photos](https://www.google.com/search?q=Timberline+Lodge+Mount+Hood&tbm=isch)
- **Trillium Lake Loop** — ⭐ **the money shot of the whole trip.** A flat, easy **1.9-mi loop**
  around a lake that mirrors Mt. Hood perfectly on a calm day. Bring the good camera.
  📷 [photos](https://www.google.com/search?q=Trillium+Lake+Mount+Hood+reflection&tbm=isch)
- Overnight **Government Camp** (on the mountain) or push toward Bend.

### Day 4 — McKenzie River waterfalls → Bend
- Drive the **McKenzie Highway** and stop at the **Sahalie & Koosah Falls Loop** — an easy
  **2.6-mi loop** with two thundering waterfalls and impossibly blue water. One of Oregon's
  prettiest short walks. 📷 [photos](https://www.google.com/search?q=Sahalie+Falls+Koosah+Falls+Oregon&tbm=isch)
- Optional add-on: **Tamolitch "Blue Pool"** — a surreal turquoise spring pool (the popular
  view is ~4 mi round-trip, mostly gentle — doable, but the longest walk of the trip).
  📷 [photos](https://www.google.com/search?q=Tamolitch+Blue+Pool+Oregon&tbm=isch)
- Arrive **Bend** for the night — sunny high-desert town, great food, easy riverfront strolls.

### Day 5 — Smith Rock + Bend
- **Smith Rock State Park** — dramatic ochre canyon walls above a river bend, an Oregon icon.
  Do the flat **River Trail** along the water for the views; **skip the steep Misery Ridge**
  (aptly named). 📷 [photos](https://www.google.com/search?q=Smith+Rock+State+Park+Oregon&tbm=isch)
- Afternoon: **Tumalo Falls** (waterfall a short walk from the lot) or a relaxed stroll through
  Bend's **Old Mill District** along the Deschutes.
  📷 [photos](https://www.google.com/search?q=Tumalo+Falls+Bend+Oregon&tbm=isch)
- Overnight Bend.

### Day 6 — Central Oregon buffer day (flex)
A cushion day — nice to have with parents so nothing feels rushed, and a smoke/weather buffer.
Options: **Newberry Volcanic Monument & Lava Lands**, the artsy town of **Sisters**, a
**Deschutes River float**, or just a slow café-and-shops day in Bend.

### Day 7 — Fly home
- Redmond (**RDM**) is ~20 min from Bend. Fly **RDM → ATL** (1 connection). Home that evening.

## 🔼 Want more? Two easy upgrades

**Upgrade A — Add Crater Lake (8–9 days, fly out of Medford/MFR instead).**
From Bend it's ~2 hrs south to **Crater Lake** — the deepest, bluest lake in the U.S., ringed by a
33-mile **Rim Drive** with overlook after overlook. Easy walks like **Sun Notch** (0.8 mi) and
**Discovery Point** (2.2 mi, flat) deliver enormous views. Mid-September is prime: stable, clear,
golden light, thin crowds. Then fly out of **Medford (MFR)**. This is the single biggest "wow" you
can add. 📷 [photos](https://www.google.com/search?q=Crater+Lake+Oregon+rim+drive&tbm=isch)

**Upgrade B — Add the Oregon Coast (2 days, keep flying out of PDX).**
Swap the open-jaw for a loop and tack on **Cannon Beach** — **Haystack Rock** rising 235 ft out of
the surf, with **Ecola State Park**'s cliff viewpoints just north (a 2-min walk from the car to one
of the most photographed views on the coast). ~90 min from Portland, and the ocean air is your
reliable **smoke backup**. 📷 [photos](https://www.google.com/search?q=Cannon+Beach+Haystack+Rock+Ecola+State+Park&tbm=isch)

## 📋 Quick booking checklist
- [ ] **Flights:** ATL→PDX in; RDM→ATL out (open-jaw / "multi-city" search). Compare vs. MFR if
      adding Crater Lake.
- [ ] **Car:** one-way SUV, PDX pickup → RDM (or MFR) drop.
- [ ] **Lodging (2–3 nights each):** Hood River → Bend, plus first night near Portland/Troutdale.
      Book early — even post-Labor Day, Hood River & Bend fill on weekends.
- [ ] **Multnomah permit:** only if you end up going before Sept 8 — [recreation.gov](https://www.recreation.gov/timed-entry/10089144), 14 days out, 7am PST.
- [ ] **Smoke check:** [oregonsmoke.org](https://www.oregonsmoke.org/) in the days before you go; keep the Coast as plan B.
- [ ] Layers + rain shell (Gorge can be misty even in summer), good shoes, real camera + polarizer
      for the waterfalls and lake reflections.
